# Provenance and proof ancestry

This document records the mathematical sources and antecedents of the proof of
Carlet's Kasami cyclic-additive conjecture formalized in this repository. It
covers theorem-level provenance and broader proof patterns when those patterns
have a recognizable literature lineage.

When using AI tools where it's difficult to clearly trace what work
they've absorbed, it's even more important than usual to ensure that
we fairly credit possible precedents.  We've tried to err on the side of
mentioning plausible parallels.  That said, the absence of a reference below
should not be interpreted as a novelty claim on our part.  We'll add
additional references as they're identified before publication.

The proof draws on four especially important strands:

1. Kasami/Müller--Cohen--Matthews/Dickson theory for the derivative image;
2. Dillon--Kashyap multiplicative phase information;
3. Fourier/Weil-moment conversion of character sums into root counts;
4. Nagy--Vajda's monomial/root-count reduction, geometric reinterpretation,
   and exact-average strategy.

The even-dimensional branch additionally uses standard degree-three Hessian
isogeny geometry and elementary arithmetic in the commutative Frobenius
subring generated by the characteristic-two Frobenius endomorphism.

## 2. Core bibliographic anchors

### 2.1 Kasami

Tadao Kasami,
*The weight enumerators for several classes of subcodes of the second order
binary Reed--Muller codes*,
*Information and Control* **18** (1971), 369--394.

This is the classical source behind the Kasami exponent

$$
d=2^{2k}-2^k+1.
$$

In modern Walsh/cross-correlation language, Kasami's theorem gives the
three-valued spectrum associated with this exponent under the usual gcd
hypotheses.

A later proof particularly close to the finite-field language used here is:

Hans Dobbertin,
*Another Proof of Kasami's Theorem*,
*Designs, Codes and Cryptography* **17** (1999), 177--180.

Kasami's classical spectral theorem should be distinguished from Carlet's
later cyclic-additive condition, which is a stronger triple-correlation
statement.

### 2.2 Carlet and the NSUCRYPTO problem

Claude Carlet,
*Componentwise APNness, Walsh uniformity of APN functions, and
cyclic-additive difference sets*,
*Finite Fields and Their Applications* **53** (2018), 226--253.
Preprint: IACR ePrint 2017/528.

Carlet introduced the cyclic-additive difference-set framework and the exact
triple-count condition proved here. In the notation of this repository, for a
set $\Delta$ of size $2^{n-1}$ the target is

$$
\left|\{(x,y,z)\in\Delta^3:
  v_1x+v_2y+(v_1+v_2)z=0\}\right|=2^{2n-3}
$$

for all distinct nonzero $v_1,v_2$. Carlet left the Kasami instance open:
his Table 2 records componentwise Walsh uniformity of the Kasami functions only
as a computation for $n\le 11$, the text states that his proof for odd
dimension "is not yet complete" and that even dimension needs another method,
and his concluding list of open questions asks for a direct proof of the
property for the Kasami functions.

This is Carlet's Definition 4.12 ("cyclic-additive difference set with
Singer-like parameters"), stated there for any $\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2^n}$
of size $2^{n-1}$, and it is the property named in his Proposition 4.11. The
formalized statement matches it term by term: the same set
$\Delta_F=\{F(x)+F(x+1)+1\}$ taken in the single derivative direction $1$,
the same coefficient restriction $v_1,v_2\neq 0$, $v_1\neq v_2$, and the same
target $2^{2n-3}$. Carlet's Remark 4.13 records that the property is invariant
under complementation and under scaling $\Delta$ by a nonzero constant, which
reconciles the formulations phrased for $\Delta_F$ and for its complement, and
makes the choice of derivative direction immaterial.

Two scope points are worth keeping distinct. Definition 4.12 is a property of
$\Delta$ alone and carries no parity hypothesis; that is what is proved here,
for every admissible $n$ and $k$. Proposition 4.11, which identifies the
property with componentwise Walsh uniformity, additionally requires $F$ to be
an APN *permutation*, hence $n$ odd; for $n$ even the Kasami exponent has
$\gcd(d,2^n-1)=3$ and that equivalence is unavailable. This repository claims
only Definition 4.12.

The Kasami case was subsequently posed as an open problem at NSUCRYPTO 2019.
A published account is:

A. Gorodilova, N. Tokareva, S. Agievich, and coauthors,
*On the Sixth International Olympiad in Cryptography NSUCRYPTO*,
*Journal of Applied and Industrial Mathematics* **14** (2020), 623--647,
DOI 10.1134/S1990478920040031.

Nagy--Vajda identify this as the venue for the conjecture and note that the
individual proposer was not publicly disclosed. Thus the statement has both a
Carlet lineage, through the 2018 cyclic-additive framework, and an NSUCRYPTO
lineage as the explicitly posed Kasami problem.

### 2.3 Müller--Cohen--Matthews, Dobbertin, and Dempwolff--Müller

P. Müller,
*New examples of exceptional polynomials*,
in *Finite Fields: Theory, Applications and Algorithms*,
Contemp. Math. **168** (1994), 245--249.

Stephen D. Cohen and Rex W. Matthews,
*A class of exceptional polynomials*,
*Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* **345** (1994), 897--909.

These papers are the source of the exceptional/permutation-polynomial family
now usually called the Müller--Cohen--Matthews (MCM) polynomials.

Hans Dobbertin,
*Kasami Power Functions, Permutation Polynomials and Cyclic Difference Sets*,
in *Difference Sets, Sequences and their Correlation Properties*,
NATO ASI Series C **542** (1999), 133--158,
connects the Kasami derivative image with the MCM permutation-polynomial
framework.

J. F. Dillon and Hans Dobbertin,
*New cyclic difference sets with Singer parameters*,
*Finite Fields and Their Applications* **10** (2004), 342--389,
provides further MCM/Dickson and difference-set ancestry and includes a short
proof of the relevant MCM permutation property.

Ulrich Dempwolff and Peter Müller,
*Permutation polynomials and translation planes of even order*,
*Advances in Geometry* **13** (2013), 293--313,
DOI 10.1515/advgeom-2011-050,
provides a modern treatment of the truncated-trace and permutation-polynomial
machinery closely related to the MCM step used here.

There is also a prior Lean formalization of substantial classical Kasami APN
and AB machinery:

Gábor P. Nagy and Attila Vajda,
`kasami-apn-ab`, commit
`a92ff580e68a328d65515a16041763bbcb67d1c6`.

That development formalizes, among other ingredients, Dempwolff--Müller
permutation machinery, the Artin--Schreier/MCM treatment of the Kasami
derivative, Walsh-moment arguments, and Frobenius transport between
complementary exponents. It does not formalize Carlet's cyclic-additive
triple-count conjecture or the Fermat-cubic/isogeny completion used here.

Their companion Lean formalization of the current conjecture paper is recorded
in `conjecture-on-kasami` at pinned commit
`aeb05ee1efe0e3446e5fb1dfaba5a43f01720713`.

### 2.4 Dillon--Kashyap

J. F. Dillon and Neeraj Kashyap,
*Jacobi-like sums and difference sets with Singer parameters*,
*Australasian Journal of Combinatorics* **55** (2013), 49--63.

Their Theorems 1--2 give the multiplicative Gauss-sum phase formula used as the
analytic interface in this formalization:

$$
\widehat F(\chi)
= \frac{G(\chi)G(\chi^{2^k+1})}{G(\chi^3)}.
$$

The sign convention is adjusted because the derivative image used here is the
complement of the difference set in their normalization.

The broader use of additive Fourier analysis to obtain multiplicative
difference-set information belongs to Dillon's earlier program; see

J. F. Dillon,
*Multiplicative Difference Sets via Additive Characters*,
*Designs, Codes and Cryptography* **17** (1999), 225--235.

### 2.5 Nagy--Vajda

Gábor P. Nagy and Attila Vajda,
*On a conjecture on the Kasami APN function: reductions, structure theorems, a
proof for $k\bmod n\in\{1,2,n-2,n-1\}$, and exhaustive verification for
$n\le13$*,
arXiv:2608.18584 (2026).

Their paper is the immediate antecedent for several structural features of the
present proof. It establishes the conjecture for

$$
k\bmod n\in\{1,2,n-2,n-1\},
$$

and verifies it computationally for $n\le 13$. Of particular relevance here,
it develops:

- Frobenius equivalence between complementary exponents;
- the MCM description of the derivative image;
- monomial/root-count reductions of the character-sum correction;
- the pointwise-lower-bound plus exact-global-average strategy;
- a geometric reinterpretation of the $k=2$ root equation on the Fermat cubic
  in Remark 11.13.

The distinction in the last item matters. Their proof of the $k=2$ case runs
through the root count

$$
\left|\{\tau:(a\tau^5+b)^3=(\tau^3+1)^5\}\right|=1
$$

on the parameter curve $a^6+b^6=1$. After Theorem 11.11, Remark 11.13 gives
the equivalent Fermat-cubic interpretation, with an exponent-five incidence
relation. The formalization here promotes that geometric reinterpretation to a
central mechanism and extends it to general admissible $k$.

Nagy--Vajda, Theorem 11.9 (the monomial reduction), is the
$k=2$, odd-dimensional specialization of the phase-to-root-count mechanism
used below. Their Section 9, especially Lemma 9.1 (the Monomial Lemma), is the
broader antecedent for the fibration/root-count reduction.

### 2.6 Tang

Chunming Tang,
*Infinite families of 3-designs from APN functions*,
*Journal of Combinatorial Designs* **28** (2020), 97--117.

Tang gives the affine 3-design interpretation of the same type of triple-count
uniformity. His Theorem 8 relates constancy of the three-variable solution
count to constancy of the corresponding cubic Walsh correlation, providing a
useful combinatorial interpretation of Carlet's condition.

## 3. The derivative image and half-size

### 3.1 Complementary parameter normalization

The formalization transports between $k$ and $n-k$ by Frobenius and then
reduces to an odd parameter satisfying

$$
2^k+1=3m,
\qquad
\gcd(m,2^n-1)=1.
$$

The Frobenius equivalence $k\leftrightarrow n-k$ appears explicitly in
Nagy--Vajda, Lemma 3.5. Dillon--Kashyap likewise note in the proof of their
Theorem 2 that the corresponding functions/sets are related by a Frobenius
automorphism. The same transport phenomenon is also formalized in the
Nagy--Vajda `kasami-apn-ab` development cited above. The additional
coprimality statement is elementary arithmetic.

Relevant Lean declarations include
`complementFrobeniusEquiv`,
`kasamiExponent_complement`, and the normalization lemmas used by
`Assembly/Reduction.lean` and `Assembly/Normalization.lean`.

### 3.2 Artin--Schreier/MCM parametrization

The formalization uses

$$
\delta(b)=M_k(b^2+b),
\qquad
\Delta=M_k(\mathrm{AS}(K)).
$$

Nagy--Vajda, Lemmas 3.2--3.3, give this identity in essentially the same
normalization. Dobbertin's 1999 NATO chapter is the earlier substantive source
for the Kasami/MCM image connection, while Cohen--Matthews is the source for
the exceptional-polynomial family itself. The Artin--Schreier/MCM step also
appears in the Nagy--Vajda Lean development.

The Artin--Schreier trace-zero description is standard finite-field theory.
Relevant Lean declarations are in `MCM/Halfspace.lean`.

### 3.3 MCM permutation theorem

The relevant MCM polynomial permutes $\mathbf F_{2^n}$ under the required
odd/coprime hypotheses. The family originates with Müller and
Cohen--Matthews; Dillon--Dobbertin later gave a short proof of its
exceptionality, and Dempwolff--Müller provide closely related modern
permutation-polynomial machinery.

The formalization proves the needed permutation theorem directly in
`mcmMap_bijective_of_odd`, using the Dickson/character-sum
machinery developed in the repository.
The elementary Frobenius-sum packet supporting its zero-freeness step is
formalized separately in `MCM/FrobeniusSum.lean` and is shared with the
Fourier route.

### 3.4 Half-size

The conclusion

$$
2|\Delta|=|K|
$$

is also an immediate consequence of the APN property of the Kasami function:
the derivative has fibres $\{b,b+1\}$. The difficult classical APN input is
part of the Dobbertin/MCM lineage.

A later direct APN proof is:

Claude Carlet, Kwang Ho Kim, and Sihem Mesnager,
*A direct proof of APN-ness of the Kasami functions*,
*Designs, Codes and Cryptography* **89** (2021), 441--446.

The proof formalized here obtains half-size through the Artin--Schreier
half-space and the MCM permutation theorem, followed by complementary-exponent
transport. The main declaration is `kasami_half_size`.

## 4. Dickson-polynomial machinery

The identities and permutation criteria for Dickson polynomials used in the
MCM analysis are classical. Standard references include Rudolf Lidl and Harald
Niederreiter, *Finite Fields*, and Rudolf Lidl, Gary L. Mullen, and Günter
Turnwald, *Dickson Polynomials* (1993).

Useful modern structural references include:

Antonia W. Bluher,
*Permutation properties of Dickson and Chebyshev polynomials with connections
to number theory*,
*Finite Fields and Their Applications* **76** (2021), 101899;

Kwang Ho Kim and Sihem Mesnager,
*Solving $x^{2^k+1}+x+a=0$ in $\mathbf F_{2^n}$ with
$\gcd(n,k)=1$*,
*Finite Fields and Their Applications* **63** (2020), 101630;

Claude Carlet and Stjepan Picek,
*On the exponents of APN power functions and Sidon sets, sum-free sets, and
Dickson polynomials*,
*Advances in Mathematics of Communications* **17** (2023), 1507--1525.

The latter two are particularly close to the Kasami/MCM setting and make the
MCM--Dickson interaction explicit.

The formalization's exact sparse Dickson and character-sum identities are
proved in `MCM/DicksonPermutation.lean`, `MCM/DicksonPhase.lean`, and related modules.

## 5. Multiplicative phase and Walsh triple counts

### 5.1 Phase formula

The proposition `DillonKashyapPhaseFormula` records the published
Dillon--Kashyap identity. `phase_formula_from_half_size` derives
the required formula internally from the half-size statement and the
MCM/Dickson identities. `Corollaries/LiteratureRecovery.lean` records the bridge between
the literature-facing formulation and the internally derived statement.

Thus the final theorem uses the Dillon--Kashyap formula as mathematical
ancestry and interface, while its Lean dependency is discharged within the
repository.

### 5.2 Walsh triple-count formula

The expression of the slope triple count as a baseline plus a Fourier
correction is standard additive-character orthogonality/Fourier inversion.
The specialization is formalized in `Counting/Average.lean`, `MCM/Fourier.lean`,
`MCM/CharacterArithmetic.lean`, and `Assembly/GeometricChain.lean`, with character infrastructure
in `Phase/AdditiveCharacter.lean`, `Phase/CharacterSums.lean`, and
`Preliminaries/FiniteFieldSums.lean`. The former supplies the canonical
primitive character and its nontriviality; the latter two supply the shared
unit-sum, principal-Gauss-sum, and characteristic-two Gauss-product identities
consumed by the root-count and internal phase derivations.

### 5.3 Exact slope average

The exact average of the slope triple counts is a direct double-counting
identity. Nagy--Vajda use the same global-average mechanism in their treatment
of the conjecture. The formalization is in `Counting/Average.lean` and
`Preliminaries/FiniteAverage.lean`.

## 6. Phase-to-root-count conversion

The formalization converts the phase correction into twisted algebraic root
counts. In schematic form,

$$
Z(A^3)
= \frac{Q^2}{8}
\left(
\sum_{u,v\in\mu_3}R_{u,v}(A,B)-|\mu_3|^2
\right).
$$

Nagy--Vajda, Theorem 11.9 (the monomial reduction), gives the exact $k=2$,
odd-dimensional specialization. Their Section 9, especially Lemma 9.1, is a
broader antecedent for the fibration/root-count reduction.

The conversion also belongs to the classical Weil-sum moment tradition. For a
standard binomial Weil sum, the third moment is expressed as $q^2$ times a
root count. Relevant sources include:

Tor Helleseth,
*Some results about the cross-correlation function between two maximal linear
sequences*,
*Discrete Mathematics* **16** (1976), 209--232;

Daniel J. Katz,
*Weil sums of binomials, three-level cross-correlation, and a conjecture of
Helleseth*,
*Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* **119** (2012), 1644--1659.

The identity formalized in `Phase.phase_to_root_count` is a
slope-sensitive, $\mu_3$-twisted trilinear version of this
moment-to-root-count calculation.

The associated cube-support collapse is an elementary cubic-character orbit
orthogonality consequence of the Dillon--Kashyap phase formula; it is
formalized by `Phase.S_eq_zero_of_not_cube` and
`phaseTripleSum_eq_zero_of_not_cube`.

## 7. Fermat-cubic incidence

The root-count problem in this formalization is converted into geometry on

$$
E:W^3+T^3=1.
$$

The direct antecedent is Nagy--Vajda's geometric reinterpretation in Remark
11.13. Their actual $k=2$ proof first establishes the relevant root count on
the parameter curve $a^6+b^6=1$; after Theorem 11.11 they observe that,
after squaring the data, it can be expressed on the Fermat cubic through the
exponent-five incidence relation

$$
W^5+pT^5=q.
$$

The formalization takes this reinterpretation as the geometric organizing
principle and uses the corresponding normalized relation

$$
\Phi_k(Q)=-(Q+\pi^kQ)+T_3
$$

for general admissible $k$, deriving

$$
W^{2^k+1}+pT^{2^k+1}=q.
$$

The relevant Lean modules are under `KasamiCyclicAdditive/FermatCubic/`, with
the final odd/even assembly in `Assembly/GeometricChain.lean` and `Geometry/EvenCase.lean`.

### 7.1 Hessian addition law

The affine addition formulas used for the Fermat/Hesse cubic are the classical
Cauchy--Sylvester Hessian addition law. A convenient explicit source is

D. V. Chudnovsky and G. V. Chudnovsky,
*Sequences of numbers generated by addition in formal groups and new
primality and factorization tests*,
*Advances in Applied Mathematics* **7** (1986), 385--434,
especially p. 425, equation (4.21i), where the formula is attributed to
Cauchy and Sylvester.

A modern secondary reference is the Hessian-curves chapter by Christophe
Doche and Tanja Lange in the *Handbook of Elliptic and Hyperelliptic Curve
Cryptography*. The Explicit-Formulas Database records equivalent Hessian
addition formulas as well.

In characteristic two, the signs in the classical affine formula collapse to
the `hessX/hessY/hessD` normalization used in the formalization.

### 7.2 Finite-field Lattès and endomorphism methods

There is direct methodological precedent for replacing finite-field questions
about polynomial or rational maps by kernel and image arithmetic for
elliptic-curve endomorphisms. Ömer Küçüksakallı,
*Value sets of Lattès maps over finite fields*, *Journal of Number Theory*
**143** (2014), 262--278, gives an alternative treatment of Dickson
polynomial value sets using a singular cubic and then extends the method to
Lattès maps induced by complex-multiplication elliptic-curve isogenies. With
Frobenius represented by an endomorphism $\pi$, finite-field points are
controlled by the modules $E[\pi^m-1]$ and $E[\pi^m+1]$, and value-set and
permutation questions become kernel/image questions for the inducing
endomorphism.

Gaëtan Bisson and Mehdi Tibouchi,
*Constructing Permutation Rational Functions from Isogenies*, *SIAM Journal on
Discrete Mathematics* **32** (2018), 1741--1749, similarly reduce permutation
properties of rational functions induced by elliptic-curve isogenies to the
interaction of the isogeny kernel with Frobenius.

The Fermat-cubic argument here is not an application of either result and does
not identify the root-count map itself as a Lattès map. The relevant ancestry
is methodological: lift a finite-field problem to an elliptic curve, express
rationality through Frobenius, and replace the original equation by
kernel/image arithmetic in the endomorphism ring. This is the pattern used
below for the rational kernel of $1+\pi^k$ and, in even dimension, for the
factorization $1+\pi^k=(1+\pi)G$.

## 8. Rational kernels and the odd-dimensional branch

For an elliptic curve over $\mathbf F_q$ with Frobenius $\pi$, the standard
fixed-point theorem gives

$$
E(\mathbf F_{q^r})=\ker(\pi^r-1).
$$

A convenient reference is Joseph H. Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic
Curves*, 2nd ed., the proof of Theorem V.1.1, where the rational points are
identified with the kernel of Frobenius minus the identity. Corollary III.5.5
supplies the separability criterion used in that discussion, but is not itself
the fixed-point statement.

The formalization specializes this directly to the Fermat cubic and proves the
needed classification of the rational kernel of $1+\pi^k$. For odd `n`, the
kernel is trivial; consequently the relevant map is injective and therefore
bijective on the finite group `E(K)`. Combining this with the Fermat incidence
gives `rootEquationSolvable_odd`.

This branch therefore combines the standard Frobenius fixed-point principle
with the Fermat-cubic reinterpretation originating in Nagy--Vajda, Remark
11.13.

## 9. The even-dimensional quotient argument

For even `n`, the proof factors

$$
1+\pi^k=(1+\pi)G
$$

inside the commutative quadratic subring generated by Frobenius. The full
supersingular endomorphism ring need not be used: all endomorphisms occurring
in the argument are polynomials in $\pi$.

### 9.1 Factorization in the Frobenius subring

For the Fermat cubic in characteristic two,

$$
\mathbf Z[\pi]\cong\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-2}],
\qquad \pi^2=-2.
$$

For odd $k=2r+1$, write $s=(-2)^r$. Then

$$
G=\frac{1+s\pi}{1+\pi}
 =\frac{1+2s}{3}+\frac{s-1}{3}\pi,
$$

and

$$
N(G)=\frac{2^k+1}{3}=m.
$$

The companion factor gives $HG=[m]$. This is standard quadratic
CM/Frobenius arithmetic together with Bézout inversion on a finite abelian
group. `Geometry/IsogenyFactor.lean`, `Geometry/PointFrobenius.lean`, and
`Geometry/FrobeniusAnnihilator.lean` formalize the arithmetic needed here.

The useful structural principle is to invert the prime-to-three factor `G`
over `K` before taking any geometric preimage under the residual degree-three
map.

### 9.2 The degree-three Hessian quotient

The explicit quotient coordinates used in `Geometry/FermatCubic/Quotient.lean` are
closely related to the standard degree-three Hessian isogeny. A direct source
is

Fouazou Lontouo Perez Broon, Thinh Dang, Emmanuel Fouotsa, and Dustin Moody,
*Isogenies on twisted Hessian curves*,
*Journal of Mathematical Cryptology* **15** (2021), 345--358,
DOI 10.1515/jmc-2020-0037, Theorem 1.

Their degree-three Hessian isogeny specializes in characteristic two to the
Fermat/Hesse cubic. After the natural cyclic coordinate change, its kernel is
the three points at infinity, which are exactly $\ker(1+\pi)$ in the
normalization used here.

An earlier source for explicit degree-three Hessian isogenies is

Daniel J. Bernstein, Chitchanok Chuengsatiansup, David Kohel, and Tanja Lange,
*Twisted Hessian Curves*,
LATINCRYPT 2015, LNCS **9230**, 269--294,
Theorem 5.4.

For an affine target $(x,y)$, the formalization uses the quotient invariants

$$
w=\frac{x+1}{x+y},\qquad
z=\frac{y+1}{x+y},\qquad
r=\frac{1+x+y}{x+y}.
$$

Choosing $W^3=w$ and setting $T=r/W$ gives a point $Q=(W,T)$ satisfying

$$
Q+\pi Q=(x,y).
$$

The quantities $W^3,T^3,WT$ are invariant under translation by the
three-point kernel, so the formulas are naturally interpreted as an affine
chart for the standard degree-three quotient invariants.

### 9.3 Frobenius defect and descent of invariants

If $Q+\pi Q$ is `K`-rational, then

$$
(1+\pi)(\pi^nQ-Q)=0,
$$

so the Frobenius defect lies in $\ker(1+\pi)$. This is the elementary
pointwise form of the usual kernel-valued cocycle arising in descent by an
isogeny.

The proof then uses only the invariant cubes $W^3,T^3$, which are rational
functions of the original `K`-rational quotient coordinates. This is the
standard quotient/invariant-theoretic principle of descending invariant
functions rather than a chosen geometric lift. Supporting descent arithmetic
is organized in `Geometry/Descent/Arithmetic.lean`, `Geometry/BaseChange.lean`, and the finite
character/power-map infrastructure.

Together these observations yield the even-dimensional root-existence theorem
in `rootEquationSolvable_even` (`Geometry/EvenCase.lean`).

## 10. From root existence to the exact count

For each cubic twist, a rescaling of a `RootEqSolvable` instance produces a
root of the corresponding twisted equation. This is the same
multiplicative-fibre philosophy as the monomial reduction in Nagy--Vajda,
Section 9.

The resulting root counts are at least one, so the phase correction is
nonnegative and

$$
N(\rho)\ge |K|^2/8.
$$

Independently, the half-size identity gives the exact slope average

$$
\mathrm{avg}_\rho N(\rho)=|K|^2/8.
$$

Hence every slope count equals the target value. This division of labor—local
geometric existence and an independent global average—is already visible in
Nagy--Vajda and is the final organizing principle of the proof here.
